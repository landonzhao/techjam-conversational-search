# Shadow Private-Evaluation Suite — Design (for review)

> A compact, high-information synthetic suite that approximates the *behavioural capabilities* the
> hidden 800-session private evaluation likely tests — designed adversarially to separate genuinely
> robust conversational shopping systems from ones that overfit the public 200. **This document is a
> methodology proposal + 10 representative sessions for review. The full 60–100-session suite is NOT
> generated yet, and no production code is changed.**
>
> We do **not** claim to reconstruct the private set. We infer the capabilities a competent judge
> would vary, from the challenge description, the four pillars, the judging criteria, the evaluator's
> behaviour, the public sessions, our architecture, and our known failure modes.

---

## 0. Critical methodology constraint (read first)

The official evaluator (`evaluator/local_evaluator.py`) **synthesizes** shopper turns; it does not
replay scripted conversations. It builds `intent_card = {target_category, hard_constraints,
soft_preferences}` and drives the loop: `initial_message` (category + first hard constraint) → agent
asks `ask_attribute` → `customer_reply` discloses the next constraint whose `classify_constraint()`
matches the asked attribute. The only behavioural variations it supports are `intent_override` (one
scripted override turn) and `boundary` (one "no preference, use your judgment" turn).

**Implication:** several requested capabilities cannot be expressed as official-evaluator samples —
free multi-turn incremental disclosure we control, arbitrary revision (color/category/size/material),
browsing→buying evolution, distractor sentences, and paired "should-NOT-clarify" cases. Therefore the
shadow suite uses a **scripted-conversation format** (§7), played by a small dedicated **replay
harness** (to be built after this methodology is approved — *not* production code, and *not* the
official evaluator). The harness feeds `turns[].user` verbatim and measures the same metrics
(first-top-10 turn → MRR/Hit@10, turns → MTTC). Cases that *are* expressible on the official evaluator
are tagged `official_compatible: true` so we can cross-check a subset against the real metric.

This split is itself a finding worth presenting to judges: the private set can test dialogue
behaviours the public harness structurally cannot, which is exactly where overfit systems break.

## 1. What I think the private 800 is trying to measure

The public 200 is generated by the same `intent_card` machinery that leaks (constraints copied
verbatim from the target's catalog text). A competent judge who wants the private set to *discriminate*
would deliberately vary the axes a leak-exploiting system cannot fake. My hypotheses (not claims):

1. **Lexical-leak independence.** The public set lets a verbatim matcher win. The private set almost
   certainly includes **paraphrased needs** ("won't be ruined in a drizzle" vs "water-resistant") so a
   coverage-only ranker collapses. *This is the single highest-probability axis.*
2. **Ranking under near-duplicates.** With recall saturated (~99%), the discriminating challenge is
   **ordering the true target above plausible lookalikes** — same category, different material / use /
   audience / feature. Directly attacks MRR.
3. **Genuine dialogue state.** Multi-turn **accumulation**, **revision** (stale constraint removal),
   and **intent override/evolution** — behaviours a single-turn keyword matcher can't fake. Pillar II.
4. **Scenario/browsing retrieval.** Needs expressed as **occasions/aesthetics/activities** with *no
   product keyword*, testing whether dense retrieval adds value beyond BM25. Pillar I browsing route.
5. **Constraint semantics.** **Hard vs soft** distinction, **negative** preferences that must persist,
   and **distractor context** that must *not* become a filter. Query understanding, not keyword grabs.
6. **Clarification precision.** Both **should-clarify** (genuinely underspecified) and
   **should-not-clarify** (already sufficient) — measuring precision, not frequency. Pillar II / MTTC.
7. **Efficient convergence.** Reaching a good answer in few turns without re-asking known facts. MTTC.
8. **Category-boundary robustness.** Semantically adjacent but categorically wrong items
   (cardigan vs jacket, pendant vs necklace, tote vs backpack) — a classic dense-retriever failure.
9. **Graceful over-constraint.** Conflicting/over-narrow needs where no perfect product exists —
   correct behaviour is relaxation or clarification, never hallucinating an ASIN.

**Design consequence:** we deliberately weight the suite toward 2, 3, and 1 (our documented honest
bottleneck) and include cases our current architecture is *expected to fail* (a suite we score 100% on
is useless). We do **not** add a case merely because we have a feature for it.

## 2. Coverage matrix (target ~72 sessions)

Categories overlap; totals do not sum mechanically (each session is tagged with all relevant
capabilities). "Primary" counts are the session's headline purpose.

| Code | Capability | Primary sessions | Why it discriminates | Metric |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| A | Direct buying (multi-constraint) | 8 | high-precision buying pipeline | HR/MRR |
| B | Exploratory browsing (scenario) | 8 | dense route > BM25; no product keyword | HR/MRR |
| C | Semantic / paraphrase match | 10 | **leak independence** (top priority) | MRR |
| D | Incremental information | 6 | state accumulation across turns | MRR/MTTC |
| E | Constraint revision | 6 | **stale-state removal** (known gap) | MRR/MTTC |
| F | Intent evolution (browse↔buy) | 5 | routing not anchored to turn 1 | MTTC/MRR |
| G | Hard vs soft requirements | 6 | constraint enforcement in ranking | MRR |
| H | Near-duplicate discrimination | 10 | **attacks MRR directly** (known gap) | MRR |
| I | Clarification (should / should-not) | 6 (paired) | clarification *precision* | MTTC |
| J | Over-constrained / conflicting | 4 | graceful relaxation, no hallucination | HR/MTTC |
| K | Distractor information | 5 | understanding vs keyword accumulation | MRR |
| L | Negative preferences | 5 | negatives persist + demote violators | MRR |
| M | Category boundaries | 5 | categorically-wrong lookalikes | HR/MRR |
| N | Efficient convergence | 4 | no redundant clarification | MTTC |

Emphasis (by design): **C (10) + H (10)** are the largest primaries — our documented honest
bottleneck. E and F over-index relative to the public set because the public set barely tests them.

## 3. Difficulty distribution

Target for the full suite (discriminating, not easy-heavy):

| Difficulty | Share | ~count | Definition |
|---|---|---:|---|
| EASY | 15% | ~11 | lexical/obvious; guards against regressions on solved cases |
| MEDIUM | 35% | ~25 | one non-trivial axis (mild paraphrase, one revision) |
| HARD | 35% | ~25 | multiple axes combined; paraphrase + near-duplicate |
| ADVERSARIAL | 15% | ~11 | designed to break our current system (pure scenario, conflicting, category-boundary) |

The 10-session teaser below intentionally skews harder (1 easy / 4 medium / 3 hard / 2 adversarial) to
stress-test the methodology.

## 4. Product / category distribution

Mirror the catalog's real skew (Women-heavy) while guaranteeing department coverage:

| Department | Share | Subcategories to cover |
|---|---|---|
| Clothing | ~50% | dresses, sweaters/knitwear, jackets/outerwear, tops, activewear, bottoms |
| Shoes | ~30% | sneakers/running, walking, boots (ankle/chelsea), heels/sandals |
| Jewelry & accessories | ~20% | necklaces/pendants, earrings/studs, bracelets; bags (tote/backpack) |

Also vary recipient (self / gift / child), occasion, activity, aesthetic, budget specificity, #
constraints, disclosure timing, lexical overlap, and intent trajectory. No more than ~2 sessions per
narrow product type (avoid "20× running shoes").

## 5. Conversation-length distribution

| Length | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 turn | ~15% | single-shot buying / should-not-clarify |
| 2 turns | ~30% | one clarification or one refinement |
| 3–4 turns | ~40% | accumulation, revision, evolution (the core) |
| 5–7 turns | ~15% | longer exploration; never padded past natural convergence |

Respect the 10-turn cap; prefer conversations that converge early (MTTC realism).

## 6. Leakage-prevention rules

Applied to every `turns[].user` string:

1. **No target title copied.** Never reuse the target's title tokens as a phrase.
2. **No distinctive catalog phrases verbatim** — except in cases explicitly tagged
   `lexical_probe: true` (a small EASY minority that intentionally measures lexical retrieval).
3. **No ASIN, brand-as-answer, or price-equals-target tell.** (The public set leaks "budget around
   $<target price>"; we do *not* — budgets are round human numbers, sometimes not the target's price.)
4. **Paraphrase attributes into shopper vocabulary**: catalog "water-resistant" → "won't get ruined in
   the rain"; "lightweight/packable" → "easy to carry all day / folds into my bag"; "moisture-wicking"
   → "keeps me dry when I sweat".
5. **No unnatural attribute dumps.** A real shopper discloses 1–2 things per turn, not all six.
6. **Distractor sentences allowed and encouraged** (Category K) but must be plausible chit-chat, not
   product constraints.
7. **Held-out vocabulary check.** Reuse `evaluator/robustness.py`'s discipline: the paraphrase words
   should be **disjoint from `src.understanding.EXPANSIONS`** where feasible, so we don't grade our own
   synonym table against itself.
8. **Ground every target in a real ASIN**; verify the target actually satisfies the stated hard
   constraints, and that ≥1 named distractor is a genuine catalog lookalike.

## 7. Evaluation metadata schema (scripted format)

One JSON object per session (JSONL). **Only `turns[].user` is ever fed to the Agent**; everything else
is evaluation/debug metadata.

```json
{
  "session_id": "shadow_0001",
  "target_asin": "B08R6J137C",
  "department": "Clothing",
  "subcategory": "jacket/outerwear",
  "difficulty": "hard",
  "primary_capability": "C_semantic",
  "secondary_capabilities": ["I_clarification", "G_hard_soft"],
  "official_compatible": false,
  "lexical_probe": false,
  "user_profile": {
    "purchase_frequency": "3-4 prior purchases",
    "average_prior_rating": 4.5,
    "rating_style": "usually positive",
    "preference_tags": ["comfort", "practical"],
    "summary": "Values practical, comfortable everyday pieces."
  },
  "turns": [
    {
      "turn": 1,
      "user": "I want a light jacket I can fold into my bag, and that'll hold up if I get caught in a drizzle.",
      "intent": "buying",
      "hard": ["water-resistant", "packable/lightweight"],
      "soft": [],
      "neg": [],
      "state_expect": "need={feature: water-resistant, feature: packable}; category=jacket",
      "clarify_ok": true
    }
  ],
  "distractors": ["B0BCKZ8FP7", "B07BCP8DG5"],
  "expected_overrides": [],
  "expected_state_final": "water-resistant AND packable both active; not a heavy down jacket",
  "target_rationale": "32 Degrees packable water-resistant puffer satisfies both semantic needs without either catalog word appearing in the messages.",
  "exposes": "Coverage/lexical rankers that need the words 'water-resistant'/'packable' verbatim; and dense rankers that grab a warm packable DOWN jacket (distractor) ignoring the rain need.",
  "tags": ["semantic", "hard_soft", "near_duplicate"]
}
```

**Per-turn fields** (`hard/soft/neg/intent/state_expect/clarify_ok`) let us filter by capability and
score sub-behaviours (e.g. did negatives persist? did the revised constraint replace the old one?).
**Component-specific subsets** are just filters over `primary_capability`/`tags`:
`semantic`, `near_duplicate`, `override`(=E/F), `clarification`(=I), `buying`(=A), `browsing`(=B),
`hard_constraint`(=G), `negative`(=L), `category_boundary`(=M).

**Metrics the replay harness will collect** (mirroring the official evaluator): per turn, top-10
recommendations; first turn target ∈ top-10 → reciprocal rank / hit / turns-to-hit; aggregate MRR,
Hit@1/3/10, MTTC, plus per-capability breakdowns and per-turn state-assertion pass rates.

findings the teaser already surfaced (to be confirmed at suite scale, not acted on yet):
- **Reveal interaction:** `shadow_s9` (should-not-clarify) has the target at **pool rank 2** yet misses
  — the single-turn adaptive-reveal hold-back (K=1) never surfaced it. Single-turn sufficient-info
  cases are penalised by the MRR-protection hold-back. The `pool` column separates this from true
  ranking error; the harness measures end-to-end (what the private evaluator would see), which is
  correct, but the diagnostic column prevents misattribution.
- **Ranking bottleneck reconfirmed:** 5/8 misses are RANKING (target in pool 7–119, ranked away) —
  consistent with the oracle. `s2` (semantic) and `s10` (scenario) are deep-pool paraphrase misses;
  `s4` is the expected stale-`boot` revision failure.
- **Retrieval/understanding gaps:** 3/8 misses (`s6` no-leather tote, `s7` distractor-polluted query,
  `s8` bare "I need a necklace") never retrieve the target — query-understanding, not ranking.

This is measurement only — **no tuning against these cases**, per instruction.

## 10. Open methodology questions for review

1. **Replay harness scope.** Approve a small `scripts/replay_shadow.py` (non-production) that plays
   `turns[].user` and scores MRR/Hit@10/MTTC + per-turn state assertions? (Needed for E/F/K/L/I cases
   the official evaluator can't express.)
2. **Official-compatible subset.** Should we additionally emit the `official_compatible: true` cases as
   standard `intent_card` samples so a subset cross-checks against the real evaluator metric?
3. **Distractor grounding depth.** Verify distractors are actually retrieved into the pool (so the case
   truly tests ranking, not retrieval) at build time — add a build-time assertion?
4. **Size/balance.** Confirm the ~72-session matrix and the 15/35/35/15 difficulty split, or reweight
   toward C/H (our bottleneck) even more?
5. **Do we hold this suite out** from all tuning (use only as a final gate), or split into
   dev/gate halves to avoid overfitting the shadow suite itself?
